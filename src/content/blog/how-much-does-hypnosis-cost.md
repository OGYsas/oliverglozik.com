**How much does hypnosis cost? In the US, most hypnotherapy sessions cost between about $75 and $250, with averages of around $120 to $175 a session. In the UK, it's usually £50 to £100.**

With me, hypnosis is a package of 3 sessions for $750, online or in person.

But the price per session is only half the question. **The real question is what the problem you want to change is costing you already, and whether the person you pay can actually help you change it.** Below I share the numbers I found, what decides the price, what I think is worth paying for, and the warning signs to look out for.

## Key points: how much does hypnosis cost?

- **US:** most single sessions cost about $75 to $250. Big cities like New York are often $190 to $250.
- **UK:** most sessions cost about £50 to £100, around £70 on average.
- **Packages** of several sessions are common and usually lower the price per session.
- **At the top end**, some therapists charge $1,000 an hour, and some coaches over $1 million a year.
- **With me:** 3 sessions for $750, 60 to 90 minutes each, the same price online and in person.
- **Insurance rarely pays** for hypnosis. HSA and FSA accounts sometimes can, with a doctor's letter.
- **Free and low-cost options exist**, like student clinics and self-hypnosis.
- **The cheapest hypnotist isn't always the cheapest choice.** Experience makes a big difference.

You'll also see much bigger numbers. Some practitioners sell intensive programmes for $1,500 to $3,500 or more, and on Reddit people regularly ask whether that's a fair price. It can be, or not. That depends on what's included and on who's doing the work, which is what the rest of this post is about.

## At the top end: $1,000 an hour, and $1 million a year

**At the very top, therapists and coaches charge far more than any price list shows.**

In New York, a reporter for Vice spoke to a therapist who charges $1,000 for a 45-minute session ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/i-went-to-a-therapist-for-the-wealthy-to-find-out-how-rich-people-deal/)). And Tony Robbins, probably the best-known coach in the world, has had several private clients paying him more than $1 million a year, according to Business Insider. One of them, the billionaire investor Paul Tudor Jones, has worked with him since 1993 ([Business Insider, 2017](https://www.businessinsider.in/After-nearly-40-years-Tony-Robbins-has-boiled-performance-coaching-down-to-two-basic-steps/articleshow/61377753.cms)).

Those people aren't paying because they have to. **They pay because, to them, the change is worth more than the money.** Otherwise they wouldn't keep paying. I find that a useful way to look at any price, mine included: not "how much is an hour?", but "what is it worth to me to finally change this?"

## What I charge, and what's included

**I work in a package of 3 sessions for $750. That's $250 a session.** Each session lasts between an hour and an hour and a half, depending on what comes up for you, and the price is the same online and in person.

Before anything is booked, we have a free call to see what you want to change and whether hypnosis is the right fit. After each session you get a recording to listen to at home, and you get access to my Hypnosis Vault, a library of hypnosis recordings, so you can keep reinforcing the change between sessions.

### Why 3 sessions and not one?

I always say: **stay open to the idea that one session can be enough.** Sometimes it is. So why a package of 3?

Because in my experience, when we overcome the main problem in the first session, there are usually other things the person wants to develop in their life. Then we use the other two sessions for those. And if the first problem needs more time, we have the room for that too. I explain how I space the sessions, and how many people usually need, in [how many hypnosis sessions do I need](/blog/how-many-hypnosis-sessions-do-i-need/).

## Why hypnosis prices vary so much

**The price depends on where the hypnotist works, how long the sessions are, what's included, and above all how experienced they are.**

- **Location.** Sessions in big cities cost more, often about twice as much as in smaller ones.
- **Session length.** Some sessions last 45 or 50 minutes, others an hour and a half.
- **What's included.** A recording, support between sessions, a free call first, and follow-up all add value.
- **Single sessions or a package.** Packages usually lower the price per session.
- **Online or in person.** Online sessions are often a little cheaper, though not always.
- **Experience.** This is the big one, and the one that's hardest to see from a price list.

## The expert difference: why experience matters more than the price

**Hypnosis works with the subconscious mind. It's delicate work, and in my view you need someone who is truly experienced to do it well.**

A lot of people do a hypnosis or hypnotherapy certification. But how many of them actually use hypnosis regularly, and consistently create results for the people who come to them? When I talk to people about hypnosis, I often hear something like: *"I knew someone with a fear of flying who had a session, or a couple of sessions, and it didn't really help."*

Here's why I think that happens. In a session, difficult things can come up. **The hypnotist needs to have looked at their own life and their own limiting beliefs, so they can sit with whatever comes up and guide you through the tough moments.** That takes more than a certificate. It takes practice, and it shows in the tonality of your voice, the conviction you speak with and the confidence you pass on to the person in front of you. In my view, you need to be a certain type of person to do hypnosis.

I have more than 3,500 hours of experience working with the subconscious mind. That's what lets me take people deeper.

## What is your problem already costing you?

**Before you compare prices, it's worth asking what the problem costs you every month you keep it.**

Think about the pattern you keep running into. Being overweight. Not being able to quit smoking. An addiction you keep going back to. Each one has a price:

- **Financial stress:** cigarettes, takeaways, the things you buy to feel better, the work you can't focus on.
- **Emotional stress:** the frustration of trying again and again, and ending up in the same place.
- **The toll on your relationships:** with your partner, your family, and yourself.

Many people go to a lot of therapy sessions and, over time, understand their problem much better. That has real value, and I'm not talking therapy down. But some people stay on the level of understanding and never manage to change. **In my sessions, people go into the subconscious mind. They can look at the experiences that shaped their life, often in a negative way, and start processing them.** As cliché as it sounds, even one session can be life-changing.

When you see it like that, 3 sessions for $750 is, in my view, actually pretty cheap.

## How much does hypnosis cost to quit smoking or lose weight?

**The price per session is usually the same as for any other topic. What changes is the number of sessions, so weight loss tends to cost more in total than quitting smoking.**

Some hypnotists sell a one-off "quit smoking" session; Bark lists these in the UK at around £120. As a rough guide from my practice, smoking often changes in one or two sessions, and weight in three to six, because eating patterns tend to have several roots.

## Does insurance cover hypnotherapy?

**Usually not.** Most health insurance plans don't pay for hypnosis on its own. Some plans may cover it when a licensed doctor or psychologist uses it as part of treating a diagnosed condition, so if that's your situation, ask your insurer.

**HSA and FSA accounts (US) sometimes can.** Hypnosis can count as an eligible expense when it's used to treat a diagnosed medical condition, usually with a Letter of Medical Necessity from your doctor. It doesn't count when it's for general wellbeing ([FSA Store](https://fsastore.com/fsa-eligibility-list/h/hypnosis), [HSA Store](https://hsastore.com/hsa-eligibility-list/h/hypnosis)). The IRS rule behind this is that an expense has to be for medical care, not general health ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-addresses-questions-about-medical-expenses-related-to-nutrition-wellness-and-general-health)). Check with your plan before you count on it.

Hypnosis with me is not medical treatment, and I don't make medical claims about it, so plan on paying for it yourself.

## Red flags: when a price is a warning sign

**A high price isn't a red flag on its own, and neither is a low one. These are:**

- **Guarantees.** "100% cured in one session" is a promise nobody can honestly make. Everyone responds differently.
- **Medical claims.** Hypnosis is not a replacement for medical or psychological care.
- **Pressure.** Being pushed into a big package on the first call, before you understand what you're paying for.
- **No clear price.** You should know exactly what you pay, and what's included, before you book.
- **No real experience.** Ask how often they work with hypnosis and with problems like yours.

## Free and low-cost hypnosis options

**If $750 isn't possible for you right now, there are honest alternatives.**

- **Student clinics.** Some hypnosis schools offer sessions with supervised trainees. The Hypnosis Motivation Institute, for example, runs a programme over Zoom with free sessions, or reduced-cost ones at $35 to $55 a session ([HMI](https://hypnosis.edu/sessions/)). Keep in mind that you're working with someone who is still learning.
- **Self-hypnosis.** It's free, and you can start today. I explain the steps in [how to hypnotize yourself](/blog/how-to-hypnotize-yourself/).
- **Recordings and YouTube videos.** These can still be very powerful, if you take them seriously. Treat a recording like a real session: find a quiet time, lie down, don't let yourself be disturbed, and really put yourself into it, so you feel your feet on the steps when you're guided down a staircase instead of just listening. Do it again and again, ideally every morning and evening. Played in the background while you scroll, it won't do much.

A recording can't respond to what comes up for you the way a live session can. But taken seriously, it's a real tool, and it costs you nothing to start.

## Is hypnosis worth the money?

**It depends on the problem, on you, and on who you work with.** Results vary: some people respond more strongly to hypnosis than others, and your own willingness to change plays a big part. Nobody can honestly promise you a result.

What I can say is this: when a hypnotist consistently helps people change, you start to see how valuable it is. Compared with years of carrying the same pattern, a few focused sessions with someone experienced can be a small price.

## Is hypnosis safe?

**Hypnosis is safe for most people.** If you have a history of psychosis, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder or epilepsy, please talk to me before you book. Hypnosis is not a replacement for medical or psychological care, and I don't make medical claims about it. If you are in crisis, contact your local emergency services.

If you'd like to work with me, have a look at [how hypnosis with me works](/hypnosis/), or book a free call.
