What is box breathing? **Box breathing is a slow breathing pattern with four equal parts: you inhale for a count of four, hold for four, exhale for four and hold for four, then start again.** Picture going around the four sides of a square. It is also called square breathing, 4x4 breathing or, in yoga, *Sama Vritti*. A former Navy SEAL made it famous, but you don't need a military background to use it. **I use it day to day, in the breaks between my concentrated work sessions.** In this post I explain how to do it, how I use it, and what it can and can't do according to the research.

## Box breathing at a glance

- **The pattern:** inhale for 4, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4.
- **How long:** start with 3 to 5 minutes. Unlike 4-7-8 breathing, there is no strict limit on rounds.
- **What people use it for:** regrounding and refocusing, calming down in a moment of anxiety, settling nerves before a big moment.
- **The research:** small trials show short-term benefits for stress, anxiety and mood, but the results are not uniform.
- **My tip:** a guided video makes it easy to follow, and classical music helps you get out of your head.

## How to do box breathing, step by step

**Sit or lie down, breathe all your air out, then go around the box: in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4.**

1. **Get comfortable.** Sit with your back supported or lie down. Never do this while driving or in water.
2. **Empty your lungs.** Breathe out slowly until the air is gone.
3. **Inhale for 4.** Breathe in through your nose, quietly and smoothly.
4. **Hold for 4.** Keep the air in, without tensing your throat or shoulders.
5. **Exhale for 4.** Let the air out slowly and evenly. Mark Divine teaches this through the nose ([Divine, 2016](https://www.time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused)), and other guides use the mouth, so do what feels easy.
6. **Hold for 4.** Stay empty and relaxed until the next inhale.
7. **Repeat** for 3 to 5 minutes to begin with.

**If a count of four is too long, start on two or three.** In my breathwork certification, a count of four was described as comfortably challenging for many students, and shorter counts as perfectly fine. You should feel at ease the whole way around.

## How I use box breathing myself

**I work in concentrated blocks of 45 minutes, and in the break between them I do box breathing.** It helps me reground, refocus and reconcentrate, and then I start the next 45 minutes. That is my day-to-day use of it.

**I do it with classical music.** One challenge at the start is simply how to follow the breath: when do I hold, when do I exhale? A guided video makes that very easy, because you can see each step. Guides where a sound tells you when to inhale, hold and exhale work too.

But I noticed that if you do that too much, **you are still a bit more in your head.** That is why I prefer melodic, instrumental classical pieces with a four-beat. The music itself is soothing, and I can follow it on my own intuitive rhythm. I might miss a beat here or there, and I think that is great, because then I am not so much in my head and I can let go more. It takes me into a calmer, more concentrated space.

**I don't find the holds very challenging.** They are limited to four, after the inhale and after the exhale, which I find a very reasonable time. There are no extended holds or exhales like in 4-7-8, so in some ways box breathing might be the easier one to practise.

## Is box breathing good for anxiety?

**Box breathing can help in a moment of anxiety, and short-term relief is also where the research is strongest.** I remember a person at a retreat who had a lot of anxiety. We did box breathing together, the person felt better afterwards, and it helped calm the situation we were in.

When people are in very anxious states, **a clear instruction helps a lot.** You can talk about this and that, but it is something else to say: okay, now we are going to follow this video together for the next 10 minutes.

Box breathing is a tool for the moment, not a treatment for an anxiety disorder. If anxiety is shaping your life, please also talk to a doctor or therapist.

## Is box breathing good for focus?

**Box breathing helps me concentrate, but the research doesn't prove that it sharpens focus.** I found no study showing it. In one lab study, 30 people did a demanding task under time pressure and noise using either tactical breathing or a long exhale. Tactical breathing lowered their physical arousal more, but **they performed better on the task with the long exhale**, and they didn't feel any different between the two ([Röttger et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-020-09485-w)). So take this part as my personal experience, and test it for yourself.

## Does box breathing help athletes?

**Some athletes use box breathing to handle pressure, but I found no good evidence yet that it improves performance.** The best-known example is the NFL quarterback Aaron Rodgers, who has said he does it before games and before two-minute drives. When he guest-hosted *Jeopardy!* in 2021, he also used it to settle his nerves ([Hodkiewicz, 2021](https://www.packers.com/news/aaron-rodgers-wanted-to-do-alex-trebek-justice-as-jeopardy-guest-host)). Even he said "Not sure it worked every single time", though he did feel better afterwards ([Mitas, 2021](https://www.cheatsheet.com/news/jeopardy-aaron-rodgers-revealed-code-words-he-kept-lectern-while-guest-hosting.html)).

In a student project with 32 participants and no control group, anxiety scores fell after adding box breathing to a basketball free-throw routine ([Melzar, 2025](https://hdl.handle.net/10456/59252)). That is a promising sign for nerves, not proof of better results.

## What the research says about box breathing

**Box breathing shows modest short-term benefits, mostly in small studies.**

- **The overall picture.** A 2026 systematic review of randomised trials found short-term benefits for perceived stress, anxiety and mood, but not uniformly, and inconsistent effects on wider measures of the nervous system. The studies are few and varied, with little long-term follow-up ([Dujawara et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-026-09812-7)).
- **Five minutes a day for a month.** In a remote trial, the box breathing group's anxiety score fell by about 3.8 points after each session on a scale from 20 to 80, nearly the same as a mindfulness group. **Cyclic sighing, not box breathing, was the exercise that clearly beat mindfulness for mood.** Only around 20 people did box breathing ([Balban et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895)).
- **Compared with other techniques.** In one session with 84 students, breathing at six breaths a minute raised heart rate variability, a marker of a relaxed nervous system, **more than box breathing or 4-7-8**. No technique meaningfully changed mood or blood pressure in that sitting ([Marchant et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10484-025-09688-z)).
- **After hard exercise.** In a trial with 62 cadets, box breathing and cyclic sighing after a maximal fitness test both helped a marker of the *rest and repair* nervous system recover better than normal breathing ([Jones et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003972)).

Part of why it may work is simply the pace. Counted in seconds, one round takes 16 seconds, which is **under four breaths a minute**, and a review of 15 studies linked breathing slower than 10 times a minute with more relaxation and less anxiety ([Zaccaro et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00353)). Across all types of breathwork, the biggest review so far found a small to medium effect on stress ([Fincham et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-27247-y)).

So don't expect magic. You will read long lists of benefits elsewhere, like lower blood pressure, better digestion and a stronger immune system. I didn't find box breathing studies that show those, so I am not claiming them.

## Where box breathing comes from

**The name was made famous by Mark Divine, a former US Navy SEAL.** Divine says he developed his version to carry on his breath training after leaving the SEALs ([Divine, 2016](https://www.time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused)). Researchers note its use in the military, where it is also called tactical breathing ([Balban et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895)). In my breathwork training it was taught as *Sama Vritti*, a yogic breath whose name means "equal flow".

## Box breathing vs 4-7-8 vs slow breathing

**For me, box breathing is for concentration and 4-7-8 is for calming down.** Everybody has their own body and preferences. **Try both, and keep the one you connect with most as your fallback.** I explain the other one in [what the 4-7-8 breathing technique is](/blog/what-is-4-7-8-breathing/).

| Technique | How you breathe | What it is often used for | How long |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Box breathing** | In for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4 | Regrounding and concentration | From 3 to 5 minutes, and longer once it is easy |
| **4-7-8 breathing** | In for 4, hold for 7, out for 8 | Calming down, nerves | 4 rounds, up to 8 after a month |
| **Slow breathing** | About 6 breaths a minute, for example in for 4, out for 6, no hold | A daily practice for a relaxed nervous system | 5 to 20 minutes |

One benefit of box breathing is that **you can do more of it in one go.** For longer forms like conscious connected breathing, read [what breathwork is and the main types](/blog/what-is-breathwork/).

## How long should you do box breathing, and how often?

**Start with 3 to 5 minutes and build up slowly.** A few minutes regularly is better than pushing for long sessions. Guidance varies: the Cleveland Clinic suggests three or four rounds, once or twice a day ([Cleveland Clinic, 2021](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/box-breathing-benefits)), Mark Divine suggests at least five minutes ([Divine, 2016](https://www.time.com/4316151/breathing-technique-navy-seal-calm-focused)), and the month-long trial above used five minutes a day.

**Why do it regularly? Think of yourself as a pressure cooker.** Tension accumulates through the days. If you take conscious time, say once a day, for some concentrated breathing, you let go of that pressure on a regular basis. If you only do it when the pressure is exploding it still works, but **I recommend making it a regular thing.**

Three things get in the way:

- **Pushing the count.** Longer is not better. Only go up when your current count feels easy.
- **Tensing up in the holds.** Holding your breath can itself set off a stress response. Keep letting go of tension, or shorten the count.
- **Worrying about the count.** Missing a beat doesn't matter. Pick up the rhythm again.

## Is box breathing safe?

**Most people can try box breathing sitting or lying down, as long as it stays comfortable.** I didn't find research on who should avoid it specifically, so this is cautious general advice for breath holds.

- **If you feel dizzy,** stop, lie down and breathe normally. Don't stand up. Light-headedness can happen to beginners and usually fades with practice.
- **If you faint, you have gone too far.** Check with your doctor before you continue.
- **If holding your breath makes you more anxious,** shorten the holds or leave them out, and just breathe slowly.
- **If you are pregnant or have a heart or lung condition,** keep the breath gentle, skip or shorten the holds, and check with your doctor if you are unsure.
- **Never do it while driving or in water,** because holding your breath can make you light-headed.

Breathwork can support you, but it does not replace medical or psychological care.

## Questions about box breathing

### Why is it called box breathing?
Because it has **four equal parts, like the four sides of a box**: inhale, hold, exhale, hold.

### Is box breathing the same as Sama Vritti?
**In my training they were taught as the same practice.** *Sama Vritti* means "equal flow".

### Do Navy SEALs really use box breathing?
**The technique was made famous by former SEAL Mark Divine**, and researchers note its use in the military. I didn't find good evidence on how widely the SEALs themselves train it.

### How is box breathing different from 4-7-8 breathing?
Box breathing has **four equal counts and a hold on empty lungs**. [4-7-8 breathing](/blog/what-is-4-7-8-breathing/) has a longer hold, an even longer exhale, and a limit on rounds.
